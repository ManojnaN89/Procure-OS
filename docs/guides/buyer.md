# Buyer guide

You turn approved requests into orders.

## What you see when you log in
**Dashboard** → **My work**: **Approved requests to source**, **RFQs waiting to be sent**, **RFQs waiting for quotes**, **RFQs with quotes to compare and award**, **Approved orders to send to the supplier**, **Sent orders waiting for the supplier's acknowledgement**, **Supplier replies to check**, **Supplier messages to read**.

## Find a price
1. **Requests** → click **Check stock levels** to draft requests for stock at its reorder point, then **Create and submit**.
2. Open an approved request and click **Find suppliers**. A contract price is used when there is one. Otherwise a request for quotation is made.
3. A quotation request never goes out by itself (D42): you confirm every new one. Under **Quotations**, tick the RFQs marked "waiting for a buyer to confirm sending" and click **Confirm and send selected** (up to 50 at once), or open one and click **Confirm and send**. Reminders to silent suppliers then go by themselves.

## Quotes and supplier selection
Suppliers answer by email. Under **Quotations**, open the RFQ: each reply is read into a draft. Check it and click **Confirm this quote**, or **Dismiss**. To type one yourself click **Enter a quote**, then **Save the quote**. To print or post the request, click **RFQ (PDF)** next to a supplier: it has the RFQ number, the lines with their need-by dates, where to deliver, the terms and the date to quote by. A quote that fails a must-have is dropped, and the table says why: it delivers after the need-by date, it has expired (a quote valid until today still counts), the supplier is not active or not approved for the category, or its GST details are missing. The rest are ranked on total cost: price times quantity plus freight and other charges. GST is shown but not counted when the company claims it back. Equal totals go to the earlier delivery, then the better vendor score. The company may instead compare on a weighted score of price, delivery, quality and payment terms; its weights are a versioned company rule. When the cheapest supplier can supply only part, the app shows a **Split** row: it splits only when the saving beats the extra freight. Pick a row for each line and click **Select these suppliers**. A written reason is needed when you select anything but the recommended, cheapest qualifying quote (or the recommended split), or when fewer qualifying quotes came in than the buying route asked for. One order is made per supplier, with GST. A price far below the other quotes or the last price paid shows "Ask the supplier": check it with the supplier before selecting. When a supplier asks a question, the task carries a drafted reply you can start from; nothing is sent until you send it.

Closing, senders and validity: An RFQ closes at the end of its closing date (India time); its page says "Closes …, end of day" or "Closed on …". A first quote received after that is refused as a late bid; a supplier that quoted on time may still send a revised price. When an RFQ first goes out, its closing date is at least the usual reply time from that day. To give suppliers more time, use **Extend closing** with a later working day and a reason: every supplier asked that has not declined gets a short fixed email. An RFQ that closed with fewer quotes than it wants becomes a case in **My work** ("closed with 1 of 3 quotes wanted"), listing who answered and who did not: extend it, select from what came in with a reason, or change the line's route. Nothing is sent or awarded by itself. Each quote says how it came: "Email from …, sender checked" (our mail server confirmed the sender), "not checked", or "Entered by" you. A reply is read only when it comes from a contact registered for one of the suppliers asked; mail from any other address, or that failed our mail server's check, is not read and becomes a task: ask the supplier to send from its registered address, or register the contact under **Vendors**. A reply whose sender could not be checked is read, but always waits for you to confirm it. A quote past its validity date cannot be selected, even with a reason: ask the supplier for a renewed price (a revision). When a selected quote expires before its order is made, the selection lapses, the RFQ is open again and you get a task; nothing is given to another supplier by itself.

Rankings, briefs and paths: Once everyone asked has answered (or the RFQ has closed), the RFQ shows **Why this ranking** in plain words. When the lowest quote is above the last price paid here in the past year or the verified market price, beyond the company's thresholds, a **Negotiation brief** gives the target price and the saving, worked out by the system. Mark it **Used** or **Dismiss**; a new price from the supplier is entered as a revision and ranked again. The brief never changes a price and never mails anyone. Text marked "AI wording, checked against the figures" is the same facts reworded; every number in it is checked against the system's figures. When a contract fits a request line but the verified market price is lower beyond every threshold (and the line is not urgent), the line waits for you in **My work**: the request page shows the contract, the market and the saving. **Ask for quotes (B)** (recommended) prepares an RFQ for you to confirm; **Buy on the contract (A)** needs a reason, and its order always waits for a person (never touchless). The contract stays valid either way.

## Orders
**Purchase orders** → open the order. Lines that share a supplier, the same payment and delivery terms and the same delivery place are on one order; a different set of terms (for example a contract line and a quoted line) or a line for another location makes a second order. The delivery place is printed on the order and in the email. The tier says who approves by the total: the purchase manager up to Rs 5,00,000, above that the director. You never approve an order you made, at any value. **Touchless** orders go out without you and without an approver: contract or safe-reorder lines up to Rs 1,00,000 and the touchless limit, an active supplier, the contract within its ceiling, prices at most 2% above the estimate, the budget fine, and touchless orders turned on. Single-source and emergency lines never do. Otherwise the **Touchless check** panel says why the order went to the purchase manager. **Open the order document (PDF)** shows what the supplier gets; it goes with the email. When it is approved click **Send to supplier**. If the supplier writes about the order, the panel **Supplier messages** on the order shows what the system understood, and **Chase suppliers now** sends the reminders that are due.

## The supplier's answer
The order email has a link where the supplier accepts the order, declines it or asks for a new date, quantity or price; it may also reply by email. Nothing changes by itself: the panel **Supplier's answer** on the order lists each change, old value to new. Click **Accept**, or write a reason and click **Reject** (the supplier gets a new link). An accepted change makes a new version of the order and the requester is told; a price rise above 2% of the first approved price goes on to the purchase manager (small rises add up: 1.5% and then 1% is 2.5%, so the second is not yours); an accepted decline cancels the order and gives you a task to source the line again. The panel also shows what the supplier says it has shipped. Reminders about the delivery date go to the supplier by themselves.

## Supply promised too late, and changes from the ERP
When an order brings what a production order needs after it needs it (the need-by less the receiving buffer), you get one case per order and material request on **Exceptions** ("Supply promised too late"): "PO-... brings 50 Motor body casting 1 HP on 8 Dec, after MO-1 needs them on 5 Dec", due in two working days. Ask the supplier to deliver in time, or buy elsewhere, then **Close** it: expedited, accepted late or bought elsewhere (a note for the last two). The case closes by itself when stock or an earlier delivery covers the need. The late part is never bought again by the store check. Changes from the ERP: when the ERP cuts or cancels a production order (or a joiner plan) whose material you already ordered or sent out for quotation, you get a case on **Exceptions** (kind "Demand changes to act on"), due in two working days (setting `demand_change_case_days`), escalating to your manager. It names the order, RFQ or request and how much is no longer needed. Revise or cancel the order, or keep the goods for stock, then **Close** the case with what you did (a note is needed for "kept for stock" and "not possible"). When an order is needed earlier than the supplier promised, the case asks you to get the supplier to deliver by the new date. Requests not yet with you are cut by the platform itself.

## Change or end an order
On the order page, the panel **Order document and changes** keeps the version and its history. Every change needs a reason.

- **Change the order** → **Make a new version**: change the **delivery place**, the **payment terms** or the **delivery terms**, and per line lower the quantity, move the date, or tick **Use the contract price in force**. The supplier and the item never change on an order: cancel it and order the lines again. Tax is never typed: a new delivery place works out the GST again (IGST becomes CGST and SGST when the place is in the supplier's state, and the other way round). The delivery place cannot change once goods have arrived or the supplier has recorded a dispatch, nor to a place a contract price does not cover; the payment terms cannot change once an invoice or an advance exists. **Use the contract price in force** moves a contract line to the contract's price version in force today, for a line with nothing received or invoiced.
- What goes for approval again: a new delivery place, new payment terms, a higher price or quantity, a date moved more than the tolerance (3 days) either way from the approved date, or a higher total. A change is measured from the approved version, not the one before, so small changes add up. Anything else, the delivery terms included, keeps the approval: the supplier gets the new version by email with its document and a new link, and acknowledges that version (an acknowledgement of an older version is refused). The history says which: "approved again" with the reasons, or "approval of version 1 carries". A changed order is never touchless again. If someone changed the order since you opened it, you are told to open it again.
- **Versions**: each version with who made it and why, its approval, when it was sent and how it was acknowledged, and **PDF**: the document exactly as the supplier got it, whatever changed since. **Compare** shows any two versions side by side, the changed values marked. A version not yet sent has no document; **Open the order document** then shows it marked "DRAFT: not issued". A contract line shows the contract and the price version it was bought at; activating a newer price version changes no order already made.
- **Cancel this order**: only before anything arrives or is invoiced. Tick **Order these lines again** if you want a different supplier or price; otherwise the need is dropped. The supplier and the requester are told.
- **Close what is still to come**: after a part delivery, when the supplier will not send the rest.

## The supplier's answer
- The order email goes to the supplier's main contact with a link. To accept, decline or ask for a change the supplier presses **Email me a code** on the page: a one-time code goes only to the address the link was sent to, works once and expires in 10 minutes. A forwarded link decides nothing. If that contact has left or the supplier is not active, no code goes out: click **Send the order again** to mail a new link for the current version to the current contact.
- Accepting gives a delivery date for each line, filled with the date ordered. A date up to 3 days later is accepted and you are told which lines are later; a later date, a day the delivery place is closed, or a date in the past is refused: the supplier asks for a change instead.
- An acknowledgement is of one version. A link of an older version says which version is current and can have it emailed to the supplier again. The order page says "Waiting for acknowledgement of version 2, due ..., 1 reminder" or "Acknowledged version 2"; **Versions** shows who acknowledged each version, how (link with a code, email, or you) and the line dates.
- **Record acknowledgement**: when the supplier confirms by phone or letter, record the version on screen with a date per line and a note of how it confirmed (required). A date more than 3 days after the date ordered, or in the past, is refused; a day the delivery place is closed is recorded and you are told.
- A change the supplier asks for waits for your decision on the version it was asked on. If the order has changed since in another way, it shows **Stale** and cannot be decided: ask the supplier again. When you accept every change of an answer and its last version keeps the approval, that is the supplier's acknowledgement; a material one goes for approval again, then the supplier accepts it with link and code. Rejecting a change mails a fresh link to the order as it stands.
- An email reply is read by the supplier agent only from a registered contact. It records an acknowledgement for the version the email replies to, and only when our mail server confirmed the sender; otherwise you get a task "Record ...'s acknowledgement" to do it with one click. A mail whose sender check failed is not read: you get a task. A reply that confirms the order but gives another date is treated as a date change. A new date moves only the line the email names, and only within 3 days with no effect on stock; "all goods on 12 Dec" for an order of several lines becomes a proposed change per line for you.
- Chasers: when the current version is not acknowledged by its due day (2 working days after it was sent, on the delivery place's working days), the supplier gets a reminder with a fresh link, then one every 3 working days, at most 3 per version. After the last you get a task "... has not acknowledged ... after 3 reminders" (the purchase manager if it waits): call the supplier, record its acknowledgement with a note, send the order again, or cancel it. A new version starts the count again.

## Suppliers and contracts
**Vendors** → **Onboard vendor**: fill in the form and click **Save as draft**. The checks name any wrong PAN, GSTIN, Udyam number or bank detail. On the vendor page click **Add a document** for each paper (PAN, GST and Udyam certificates; a bank proof is filed for payment, not needed to activate) and **Attach** its scan. A different person verifies each document, and a person with approval rights activates the supplier. The bank account of a supplier already in use can only change with another person's approval. **Contracts** → **Store contract**, **Add a price**. A different person activates the contract. **Vendors** → **Register a prospect** saves a supplier that may quote before onboarding; the same PAN, GSTIN or bank account as any supplier (a blocked one too) is refused, and a shared phone, email, domain or address is flagged for review. On an RFQ, **Invite a prospect**. Its quote "does not count" and cannot be selected until someone else checks its identity; selecting it then is a conditional award (no order until it is activated; after 10 working days you choose again with a reason). A supplier or contract price may be limited to a region: other places go to quotation ("No contract here: …"). **Contracts** → **Market prices**: add or upload benchmarks (evidence only, verified by someone else). Strategic reviews land in **My work**; close each with a reason, or **Ask for a review** on a contract.

## What you cannot do, and if you are stuck
You cannot approve an order, verify a document you added, or activate a contract. An order held for a price waits for an Approver. Goods that came with no order show in **Receive without order**; once the purchase manager approves, click **Raise the order** and give the price agreed with the supplier, then send it as usual. A supplier with a missing or unverified document cannot be used: open the vendor to see which one. **Exceptions** lists everything stuck that needs a person, oldest first, with the reason, who must act, the next step and the age in working days; tick **Only mine** for what waits on you.

## Forward buying
When an item's market price is well below its trend, the request page shows **Buy more now while the price is low** with the months, quantities and savings worked out by the system and the benchmark behind them. Accept it (you may buy less, in whole purchase units) with a reason, or reject it. The extra quantity goes to the purchase manager to approve and becomes a separate request; the original request is bought as usual.

## Prices for a period (strategic sourcing)
To price items for a period with no request (a rate contract), click **Quotations** → **New period RFQ**: pick the location, the period (it starts after today and lasts at most 12 months) and the items, all of one category. **Suggest** fills each quantity with the engine's figure and says why (the open requirements in the period, or what was bought in the last 12 months for as many months); you may type your own. The quantities are an estimate, not a commitment: the suppliers' mail says so. From a strategic review on a contract, click **Start a period RFQ**: the review's items, place and period are filled in and the review is closed with the RFQ's number. Confirm sending as for any RFQ.

Enter the quotes as usual, then pick a supplier per line and click **Propose award**. Nothing is awarded yet: the award waits for the person who approves orders of its value (never you; an award is never approved automatically). Leave **Send regret notes to the others** ticked to thank the suppliers that were not chosen (the note names no price and no other supplier). You may **Withdraw** the award before it is decided. When it is approved, each chosen supplier gets an award letter with a draft rate contract to accept or decline by link and code; you are told when it does. The contract is used only after the supplier accepts it and a second person activates it; then a later request buys on it like any contract.
